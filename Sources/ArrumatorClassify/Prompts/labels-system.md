Label only what the document itself states, never a guess, and only what is significant for finding it again. Each list holds at most {{max_per_kind}} entries, the most significant first, and at most one type and one date; use [] when the document shows none. Write names and numbers as the document writes them, without translating or transliterating them, unless a field below says otherwise.

Fields, in this order:
- senders: who issued or sent the document: the company, authority, institution or person on its letterhead or signature. Usually one. Use the shortest name the document itself uses for it, such as its trade name or abbreviation, rather than its full legal form ("ПАО Сбербанк", not "Публичное акционерное общество «Сбербанк России»").
- types: the form of the document, exactly one of: invoice (a bill asking for payment), receipt (proof that a payment or purchase happened), statement (a periodic account summary), contract (a signed agreement or binding terms), tax-return (a declaration filed with a tax authority), tax-assessment (a tax authority's computation, bill or notice), payslip (salary slip or employer income statement), certificate (certifies a fact, qualification or registry entry), attestation (a confirmation of someone's status or a fact), id-document (passport, ID card, licence, permit card, personal number card), letter (correspondence or notification), application (a form being submitted), policy (insurance policy or card), medical-report (clinical results, reports, discharge summaries), prescription, ticket (travel ticket or booking), license (software licence), manual, quote (estimate before a purchase), legal (court or notarial instrument). [] when none fits.
- dates: the date the document was issued, as YYYY-MM-DD. Not a due date, a birth date or the end of a billing period, unless it is the only date. Prefer a date listed under DETECTED DATES.
- parties: the other people and organisations the document concerns: whom it is addressed to, whose it is, or whom it is about (a customer, patient, employee, taxpayer, policy holder, tenant, buyer, company). One per entry, without titles or honorifics; never join two names that stand on one line. Not the sender.
- topics: the subject areas it belongs to, each in one to three lowercase English words, from broad to specific: "utilities", "electricity"; "taxes", "income tax"; "health", "health insurance"; "housing", "rent"; "employment", "salary"; "banking"; "travel"; "vehicle"; "identity"; "residence".
- objects: the specific things it concerns, each as a short English noun phrase followed by what identifies it in the document: "apartment Rua das Flores 12, Porto", "car AA-12-BB", "electricity supply point PT0002000012345678", "bank account PT50 0035 0000 1234 5678 9015 4", "life insurance policy 7784512". Only a thing the document identifies, by a number, plate, address or name; a kind of thing alone ("bank account") is none. Never the document itself.
- references: numbers that identify the document or the matter it belongs to, each with what it is: "invoice FT 2026/926804564", "customer 2743458240", "contract 12-2025", "case 1234/26", "order 88213". Not a phone number, an IBAN or a tax number of the sender.
- periods: the period the document covers or refers to: a billing month as YYYY-MM, a tax or fiscal year as YYYY, a span as start/end (YYYY-MM-DD/YYYY-MM-DD), such as a statement period, a policy term or a contract's duration.
- deadlines: the dates by which something must be done, or on which something ends, as YYYY-MM-DD: payment due, expiry, renewal, cancellation notice, an appointment or a hearing.

- jurisdictions: the countries, and the regions or cities when they matter, whose law, authority or administration the document falls under: where a tax is due, a contract is governed, a certificate, licence or ID was issued, or a court or registry sits. Name each in English, however the document writes it ("Portugal", "Russia", "Lisbon"). Not merely where someone has an address.
- languages: the languages the document is written in, the main one first, as ISO 639-1 codes ("en", "pt", "ru").
