How to organise this personal archive (established records-management practice; the folder tree grows only as documents arrive):
1. Findability first: every document must have exactly one obvious home, and a folder's name must say what is inside, as if the user had forgotten creating it.
2. Shallow and broad: exactly two levels, Area / Category, numbered Johnny.Decimal style (the app assigns the numbers). An area holds at most nine categories and the archive at most nine areas, so keep them broad enough to last. A category may use year subfolders; never create any other deeper level.
3. Organise by function and subject — the part of life the document serves (identity, money, home, health, work, insurance, travel…) — never by file format, sender, or date. The document type (invoice, statement, contract…), the correspondent and the date are metadata kept in the file name and index, not folders.
4. Be consistent: reuse an existing category whenever it plausibly fits; do not create near-duplicates of an existing folder, and never create catch-all folders such as "Misc", "Other" or "Documents". Propose a new category only when no existing one fits, and a new area only when no existing area is a sensible parent.
5. Areas that commonly emerge in personal records, for orientation only: identity & civil status; money & taxes; home & utilities; health; work & education; insurance & legal; vehicles & travel; digital services.
6. Keep categories country-agnostic, except taxes: split them by jurisdiction (for example "Taxes (Portugal)", "Taxes (Russia)") because authorities, forms and deadlines differ.
7. Recurring or periodic documents (bank statements, utility and phone bills, payslips, tax documents, medical results, receipts) go into categories with year subfolders; one-off documents (contracts, identity documents, diplomas, policies) do not.
8. Documents from a tax authority belong with that country's taxes; an insurer's documents belong with insurance; translations and apostilles follow the original document.
9. Folder names are concise (about 2–4 words, under 32 characters), in {{folder_language}}, Title Case, ordered from general to specific, with no dates, numbers or special characters. A folder description is 1–3 sentences in {{folder_language}} saying what belongs there and what does not.
