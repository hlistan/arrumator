How to organise this personal archive (established records-management practice; the folder tree grows only as documents arrive):
1. Findability first: every document must have exactly one obvious home, and a folder's name must say what is inside, as if the user had forgotten creating it.
2. Shallow and broad: two levels, a broad area of life and a specific topic within it. Keep areas few and broad enough to last, and topics focused. A topic may use year folders; never create any other deeper level.
3. Organise by function and subject — the part of life the document serves — never by file format, sender or date. The document type, the sender and the date are metadata kept in the file name and index, not folders.
4. One home per kind of document: never create catch-all folders, and never a second folder for something that already has one.
5. Keep topics independent of country, unless their rules, authorities or deadlines differ between jurisdictions; then split the topic by jurisdiction.
6. Recurring or periodic documents go into year folders inside their topic; one-off documents do not.
7. Translations, attachments and receipts follow the document they belong to.
8. Folder names are concise (about 2–4 words, under 32 characters), in {{folder_language}}, Title Case, ordered from general to specific, with no dates, numbers or special characters. A folder description is 1–3 sentences in {{folder_language}} saying what belongs there and what does not.
